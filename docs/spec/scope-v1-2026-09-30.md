**RIVR platform: product scope and build plan**

**Summary**

RIVR needs one platform with four front ends: a player app (PWA now, store apps later), a floor and operations console that is the system of record, a dealer tablet that captures every hand, and TV displays. Advertising sync, WhatsApp and payouts run as backend services on the same database. The player app mirrors Grand Poker Arena's member app (Home, Sessions, Balance, Rewards) and adds a fifth tab, Learn, which ports the poker-settle drill engine with licensed book content.[^1]

The binding date is 30 Nov, not 1 Jan. Dealer training runs 30 Nov to 28 Dec, and dealers must train on the tablet and cage flow they will use on launch night. Police permission targets 15 Dec and the walk-through runs 23 to 29 Dec, so KYC, the cage ledger, CCTV-linked incident logging and the AML register have to be demonstrable by mid-December.

Three findings change the spec against what was asked:

• Google Ads prohibits offline gambling ads targeting India. The Google sync is built behind a flag, but no campaign should be planned on it; counsel's view on Indian law does not override Google's contractual ad policy.

• Meta allows ads for brick-and-mortar gambling without written permission only if the landing page does not promote or facilitate online gambling. Ads therefore land on a separate marketing site, never on the app screens that hold a wallet.

• Holding player balances needs money rails that will onboard a poker business. Cashfree lists gaming and gambling as prohibited, so v1 runs on UPI into NewCo's current account with UTR capture and bank reconciliation, with a payout adapter ready for a provider once one signs.

**What GPA does, what best in class does, what RIVR builds**

GPA's bundle shows a broad back office: members, check-in, waiting list, host queue, call-time, tables, sessions, buy-in requests, treasury with chip ledger, EOD reports with anomaly flags, employees, promotions, hourly rakeback, jackpots with season and freeroll pools, a spin wheel, retention offers and tournaments. KYC runs through DigiLocker on the Zoop gateway with a PAN fallback. It is a strong base; the gaps are in rake capture, promotion design, tournament tooling and identity assurance.[^2]

The US reference systems set the bar on the floor side. Genesis Gaming's Bravo runs most Las Vegas rooms and covers waitlist, player tracking, table drop analysis and tournament registration through payout. Table Tracker, a newer web-first system, adds QR self-join to the waitlist with a step-away hold, a server-side tournament clock that survives a tablet or TV dropping, automatic dealer on/off timestamps with per-table rake and hand counts, and configurable rake-back schemes. Blind Valet makes the same argument against desktop tournament software: one cloud tournament, every device a live window into it.[^3][^4][^5]

On promotions the reference is Talon.One: each campaign carries budgets, start and end dates and rules built from conditions and effects, which is the shape RIVR's rule builder takes.[^6]

**Architecture**

• One Supabase project in the Mumbai region, owned by NewCo, is the single backend. Postgres holds every record; Realtime drives the floor, tablets and TVs; edge functions run the vendor integrations; pg_cron runs scheduled jobs.

• One monorepo, four front ends, shared domain packages.

> – Player app: React PWA, installable, Capacitor-ready for store builds.
>
> – Ops console: desktop-first web app for floor, cage, marketing and admin roles.
>
> – Dealer tablet: kiosk-mode web app locked to one table, offline-tolerant.
>
> – Displays: read-only screens driven by server state, recover on refresh.

• poker-settle's conventions carry over unchanged: database access only in features/\*/api, money rules only in framework-free domain modules, golden tests on every money rule, React Query keys scoped by user, notifications through one wrapper.

Three rules are specific to a room that holds money:

• Every rupee moves through a double-entry ledger. A balance is a sum of entries, never a stored number, and no entry is edited; corrections are reversing entries.

• Writes that must not double (buy-ins, cash-outs, hand records, payouts) carry an idempotency key, so a retried tap or a replayed offline write lands once.

• Every privileged action writes an audit row with actor, device, IP and before/after state; cage actions above a threshold need a second approver.

**Floor and operations console**

Sixteen modules. Each lists scope and the test for done.

**1. Members and KYC**

• Mobile OTP sign-up (WhatsApp authentication template, SMS fallback), DigiLocker pull for Aadhaar and PAN, PAN verification fallback, selfie liveness and face match, 18+ gate.

• Store masked Aadhaar only, the KYC photo, PAN, DOB, address and vendor reference; raw documents stay with the vendor. DPDP consent recorded per purpose.

• Tiers: member, Academy graduate, Founders' Circle, black card (with its qualifying stake), staff-assigned with reason and expiry. Self-exclusion and cool-off set by the player or by the floor.

• Duplicate detection on PAN, Aadhaar reference and face match.

**Acceptance test.** A new player completes KYC on a phone in under three minutes, the cage sees the KYC photo on every transaction, and an under-18 or duplicate identity cannot reach a seat.

**2. Check-in, waitlist and seating**

• Check-in at the door by QR or phone number; visit record opens and drives cab eligibility, comp tracking and Meta visit events.

• Waitlist per game and stake, joinable from the app before arrival, with live position and a 20-minute step-away hold.

> – Priority score: black card first, but only on the list for the stake his black card qualifies at; then Founders' Circle; then join time. On any other list a black-card holder queues by join time. Weights sit in config so Rahul can tune them without a release.
>
> – Interest lists open a new table when enough players are waiting.
>
> – Seat-ready call-down by app push and WhatsApp; unanswered calls roll to the next name.

• Seat map for all five tables and the private room; must-move lists; table open and close.

**Acceptance test.** A black-card player at his qualifying stake joining behind five members is seated first, the same player on a different stake's list is not, and a player who steps away keeps position for 20 minutes and no longer.

**3. Tables, games and rake rules**

• Table types carry game, blinds, buy-in range, rake % and cap: one Rs. 5,000 table, three Rs. 10,000 tables and the Rs. 20,000 private room with compulsory straddle, per the deck.[^8]

• Variation button games (Texas, PLO, bomb pot) are hand-level attributes, so rake and variance can be cut by variant.

• The Rs. 5,000 table's cap is still an open assumption in the deck; it lives in config.[^9]

**Acceptance test.** Changing a cap is a console edit with an effective time, and hands before that time keep the old rule.

**4. Dealer tablet**

• Locked to one table. Dealer signs in by PIN on push; on/off times stamp automatically.

• Per hand the dealer enters the final pot and taps the variant.

> – The tablet computes 5% rake to the cap and the jackpot drop (Rs. 100 on pots above Rs. 5,000 once the bad-beat jackpot is live) and shows the chips to pull.
>
> – High-hand claims (royal, straight flush, quads with both hole cards playing) are logged against the hand for floor approval.
>
> – Seat events: buy-in request to cage, player up, player away, player left.

• Offline queue: hands save locally and sync in order with idempotency keys, the pattern poker-settle already uses for buy-ins.

• At each down the drop-box count is entered; variance against logged rake above a threshold raises an anomaly.

**Acceptance test.** A dealer can log a hand in two taps plus the pot, the tablet survives five minutes without Wi-Fi, and the investor MIS shows rake by table, dealer and variant from hand records.

**5. Cage and wallet**

• Buy-in by cash, UPI to the NewCo account with UTR capture, or wallet debit; cash-out to cash, wallet or UPI payout. Floor sends buy-in requests to the cage, as GPA does.

• Wallet holds a cash balance and a bonus balance; bonus is playable, not withdrawable, and expires per promotion rule.

• Withdrawal requests from the app to verified payout accounts, executed by the cage with the UTR recorded; a payout-provider adapter replaces manual UTR entry when a provider onboards.

• Dual control: amounts above a configurable limit need a second cashier or the floor manager.

• TDS engine with an on/off switch.

> – Parameters: section label, rate, threshold, basis (net winnings per session, per day or per financial year), effective date, PAN requirement.
>
> – While off, the engine still computes shadow TDS on every cash-out, so a retroactive ruling becomes a report rather than a reconstruction.
>
> – Withholding ledger and quarterly return extracts; certificate data per player.

• Chip inventory by denomination, float, chips-in-play and EOD close with anomaly review, carried over from GPA's treasury.

• Credit lines to players, live at launch.

> – Limit per player set by the floor manager and approved by Rahul above a threshold; KYC and a minimum visit history required before any limit is granted.
>
> – Credit buy-in issues a marker against the limit; the player signs on the cage screen and the marker posts to a receivables account, never to the wallet.
>
> – Cash-outs and wallet balances settle open markers first (GPA's settle-credits flow), partial repayment by cash or UPI, ageing buckets, and a block on new credit past due date.
>
> – Room-level exposure cap and a daily report of credit outstanding by player and age.

**Acceptance test.** A full shift closes to zero variance between chips, cash, UPI receipts, markers and ledger, a cash-out with TDS switched on and off produces the right net and the right ledger entries, and a player with an open marker cannot cash out without settling it.

**6. Tournaments**

• Templates and a blind-structure calculator; registration and re-entry from the app or cage; late registration to a set level (level 9 for RIVR Sunday).

• Random seating, table balancing prompts, server-side clock that survives any device dropping.

• Prize pool from entries after fee (Rs. 1,000 of Rs. 10,000 for RIVR Sunday), guarantee and overlay booked to the P&L, adaptive payouts with configurable places, ICM and chip-chop deal calculator.

• Leaderboard and League points written on completion.

**Acceptance test.** A 65-entry Sunday with re-entries runs start to payout with no spreadsheet, and overlay against the Rs. 5 lakh guarantee posts to the ledger automatically.

**7. Promotions, schemes and events**

• A rule builder the floor manager can use without a developer.

> – Trigger: buy-in, hours played, hand logged (high hand, bad beat), tournament entry, referral completed, check-in, Academy graduation, date and time window.
>
> – Conditions: tier, stake, table, day of week, first N per table or per day, new or lapsed player, gender for Ladies' Night, visit count in the month.
>
> – Effects: bonus balance credit, points, freeroll ticket, cab eligibility, physical prize, jackpot payout, WhatsApp or push message.
>
> – Controls: start and end, budget cap in rupees and count, per-player limit, stacking rules, approval by a second role, and a simulation that replays last week's data before publish.

• Templates ship for every scheme in the deck.[^10]

> – 20% buy-in bonus on the first five buy-ins per table on promo day; high-hand bonus; bad-beat jackpot; referral Rs. 1,000 each side; graduate first-buy-in Rs. 1,000; RIVR Rewards at 5% of rake; Founders' Circle priority; cab for 4+ visits a month; Ladies' Night; RIVR League; Rs. 5 lakh Sunday guarantee.

• Each promotion carries a player-facing card (title, image, terms) that publishes to the app Home carousel, the TV rotation and, for opted-in players, a WhatsApp marketing template.

• Events calendar for pro nights, corporate tables and Academy batches, bookable from the app.

**Acceptance test.** Rahul's floor manager creates a new Thursday scheme, simulates it and publishes it to the app and TVs in under ten minutes, and the monthly P&L shows its cost against its budget.

**8. Jackpots and high hands**

• Bad-beat jackpot pool funded from per-hand drops, with seed, qualifying rules, split between loser, winner and table, and a season or freeroll sub-pool if Rahul wants GPA's structure.

• Pool is players' money and sits in its own ledger account, separate from revenue.

• High-hand board with floor approval, payout and history; both feed the TV and app counter.

**Acceptance test.** The pool balance on the TV equals the sum of drops less payouts to the rupee at any moment.

**9. Rewards and League**

• Points ledger earned on hours by stake or on rake, redeemable for tournament seats, F&B upgrades and merchandise; tiers with thresholds; expiry rules.

• Learn earns points per drill session completed and per principle mastered, capped per day so a player cannot farm points by re-drilling an easy chapter.

• Academy graduation assigns the graduate tier, which carries its own promotions.

• RIVR League: monthly points across cash and Sundays with a season championship.

**Acceptance test.** A player's points in the app reconcile to the ledger and to the hours logged by seat sessions.

**10. Staff and employees**

• Role-based access for 11 roles in the manpower plan; device binding and IP allowlist for cage and admin, as GPA enforces.

• Roster, shifts, attendance, dealer rotation board, tip-pool export (tips belong to dealers).

• Onboarding checklist per hire linked to SOP acknowledgements.

**Acceptance test.** The 38-person full team can be rostered for a week, and a cashier cannot open admin screens.

**11. SOPs, fallbacks and POCs**

• SOP library with owner, version, effective date and required acknowledgement by role; the December training set (dealer certification, cage, women-safety, AML, table-side F&B) is loaded first.

• Fallback runbooks for app down, Wi-Fi down, power cut, UPI down and KYC vendor down, each with the paper form to use and the reconciliation step to bring paper back into the system.

• POC directory: internal escalation matrix, vendors, Glaza, police station, fire, DHBVN.

• Incident log with severity, CCTV reference and linked SOP; quarterly drill scheduler.

• Nightly offline pack: a printable PDF of SOPs, POCs, the floor state and open balances, saved to the cage computer so the room can run on paper if the platform is unreachable.

**Acceptance test.** A simulated outage during the December mock sessions runs on paper for 30 minutes and reconciles back with no lost buy-in.

**12. AML and responsible play**

• Cash buy-in limits per visit and per day, source-of-funds flags, structuring detection (several buy-ins just under a limit), suspicious-activity register with review workflow.

• Player-set limits and self-exclusion honoured at check-in, waitlist and cage.

**Acceptance test.** A flagged pattern creates a register entry with the transactions attached, and an excluded player is refused at the door screen.

**13. Glaza reconciliation**

• Monthly CSV import of Glaza's F&B lines, matched to player visits by table and time.

• Statements: complimentary food and soft drinks at cost, alcohol margin split 50:50.

**Acceptance test.** The monthly Glaza statement ties to the import and to NewCo's payable.

**14. Transport**

• Cab eligibility computed from visits in the month; pickup and drop requests from the app; driver assignment and trip log.

**Acceptance test.** A player with four visits sees the cab option and a player with three does not.

**15. Academy operations**

• Batches, bookings and payments (Rs. 2,000; corporate Rs. 1,500 per head), attendance, graduation, and the graduate bonus and freeroll entry issued as promotion effects.

**Acceptance test.** A graduate's first buy-in carries the bonus without a manual step.

**16. Reports, MIS and retention**

• EOD close, table-level rake from hand records, dealer performance, promotion cost, cohort retention, and the monthly investor MIS that MOU B commits to.

• Host queue for lapsed players with churn reasons and offers, as GPA runs it.

**Acceptance test.** The investor MIS generates on the 1st with no manual input except the Glaza import.

**What has to change**

• Storage moves from localStorage to Postgres. poker-settle keeps source, schedule and attempts on the device, so a cleared browser wipes history and nothing syncs. RIVR stores FSRS cards and attempts per member with a local cache for offline sessions.

• Licensing becomes data. Each source carries its licence terms, state and expiry; under the author licence every KYC'd member is entitled, and the source switches off for everyone if the licence lapses.

• A lesson format is added for Academy Levels 1 to 3: short cards plus quizzes. Hand-ranking and rules quizzes reuse the engine's existing classify and count answer kinds, so Level 1 needs content, not new engine code.

• PLO and bomb pots are outside the engine. Its ontology and grader are hold'em only; variation-game drills are a later phase and need new classifiers.

• The CI gates move with the engine. Every source file runs the validator, the author's quiz hands against the pass threshold, and the generator, overclaim, feedback, variety, lines and MECE checks before it can ship.

**Displays**

• Modes: waitlist board by game and stake, tournament clock with payouts and average stack, bad-beat and high-hand board, promotions rotation, League leaderboard.

• Each TV is paired to a mode from the console, holds no state and recovers on refresh, so a power cut costs a reload, not a reset.

**Growth: ads, influencers and WhatsApp**

Meta: build and run. Meta's standards let advertisers promote brick-and-mortar gambling establishments without written permission, provided the ad or landing page does not promote or facilitate online gambling. The marketing site is therefore a separate domain with no wallet, and events flow to Meta through the Conversions API with action_source set to physical_store; the separate Offline Conversions API was discontinued in May 2025.[^15][^16]

• Events sent: Academy booking, KYC complete, first visit, first buy-in, tournament entry, repeat visit. Identifiers hashed, sent only with marketing consent, within the seven-day freshness window.

Google: built, not relied on. Google's 19 Nov 2025 policy update lists India among the countries where offline gambling ads are prohibited. The offline conversion import sits behind a flag so it can switch on if the policy changes; any Google spend in the meantime risks disapproval of the account.[^17]

Influencers: each creator gets a code and tracked links. Attribution runs from first touch to KYC, first visit and first buy-in, with a dashboard per creator and payout calculation on a configurable commission basis.

WhatsApp: Meta Cloud API direct, no reseller markup. Physical gambling establishments can message without Meta's prior permission, but messages may not facilitate payments, so templates carry seat calls, receipts-ready notices and promotions and never a payment link. Marketing templates cost 86 paise each in India from 1 Jan 2026, and from 1 Oct 2026 service messages are charged beyond 1,000 a month.[^18][^19][^20]
